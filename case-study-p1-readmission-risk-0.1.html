Predicting 30-Day Hospital Readmission Risk

Testing whether the data already captured at discharge — admission history, length of stay, diagnoses, demographics — can flag patients likely to return within a month, without lab results or clinical notes.

The question

Can structured admission data predict whether a patient will be readmitted within 30 days of discharge, using only fields already present in a standard EHR extract?

Approach

Using MIMIC-IV, a de-identified clinical dataset from a U.S. academic medical center, eleven features spanning prior utilization, diagnostic complexity, demographics, and comorbidities were used to train two models — a logistic regression baseline and an XGBoost classifier — evaluated on a held-out 20% test split.

Result

XGBoost reached a ROC-AUC of 0.698 and a PR-AUC of 0.323, roughly double the no-skill baseline set by the 15.2% readmission rate. Logistic regression was close behind at 0.689, suggesting most of the available signal is linear.

Where this model sits against published clinical tools

ROC-AUC scale, 0.60–0.80. The shaded band marks the 0.65–0.75 range typically reported by administrative-data tools such as LACE and the HOSPITAL score; both models fall within it.

What's driving the prediction

Feature attribution (SHAP, XGBoost) points to admission history as the dominant signal, with diagnostic complexity, length of stay, cancer status, and age contributing smaller amounts. These describe how the model uses each feature to rank risk — not a causal explanation of readmission.

Read with care

No laboratory values or vital signs were available, which likely caps achievable performance. Data come from a single academic medical center, so generalizability elsewhere is untested. Predicted probabilities are uncalibrated and should be read as a relative ranking, not a literal probability. This is a research prototype and has not been validated for clinical deployment.
