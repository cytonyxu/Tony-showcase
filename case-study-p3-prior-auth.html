Background

The prior authorization problem

Prior authorization is a payer requirement for physicians to obtain approval before certain procedures are reimbursed. For Medicare, coverage is governed by Local Coverage Determinations (LCDs) — dense regulatory documents specifying exactly which clinical presentations are covered, which are not, and why.

The problem is not just speed. A physician denied authorization receives a letter. The letter rarely cites the specific policy criterion that triggered the denial. Without that attribution, the denial cannot be meaningfully contested, the clinical note cannot be improved, and the system cannot be audited. Clinicians spend an estimated 14.9 hours per week on prior authorization tasks — the majority of that time is not making decisions, it is chasing justifications for decisions that have already been made.

P3 asks: can an LLM produce coverage determinations that are not just accurate, but independently auditable — every claim traced to the exact policy passage that supports it?

Architecture

Citations API, not RAG

The core technical choice for P3 is the Anthropic Citations API. This is distinct from standard retrieval-augmented generation. There is no embedding step, no retrieval index, no chunk selection. The full CMS policy document is passed as a structured document block with citations: {enabled: true}. Claude reads the policy and the clinical note together, produces a determination, and returns character-indexed citations — start_char_index and end_char_index — pointing to the exact passages in the source document that support each criterion.

What this means for auditability A reviewer can look up chars 4790–5060 in LCD L34220 and confirm whether the cited text actually contains the exclusion criterion Claude claims to have applied. The determination is not just correct — it is independently verifiable. That distinction matters for healthcare applications where accountability is non-negotiable.

Policy documents

LCD L34220 — Lumbar MRI

  • Coverage indications and red flag conditions
  • 4-week conservative management rule
  • Explicit exclusions (DDD, screening, duplicate imaging)
  • Contrast indications
  • Noridian J-E / J-F · Effective 10/23/2025

Article A57206 — Billing & Coding

  • Documentation requirements per claim
  • Attending physician order requirements
  • Utilization guidelines (single vs repeat MRI)
  • Professional component billing rules
  • Companion to LCD L34220

Both documents are public domain (CMS Medicare Coverage Database). No data use agreement required. LCD L34220 was used as the primary document for all 10 evaluation cases; A57206 integrated for documentation requirement checks.

Proactive clinical note The request is for MRI without contrast. Given the history of prostate cancer and concern for possible osseous metastasis, the LCD indicates contrast is appropriate for CNS metastatic disease (chars 5834–6737). The ordering physician should consider whether contrast should be added to improve diagnostic yield.

The proactive contrast note was not prompted. The model inferred, correctly, that a cancer patient with night pain and new back symptoms warrants a contrast discussion — and cited the specific LCD passage that supports contrast indication. This is the kind of clinical nuance that makes the output useful beyond a binary covered/not-covered answer.

Notable findings

What the evaluation revealed

  • The red flag pathway was correctly identified in every covered case. All five covered cases involved at least one red flag (cancer, progressive neurological deficit, fever, IV drug use). In each case the model correctly waived the 4-week conservative management requirement and cited the specific red flag criterion by name. No covered case was incorrectly routed through the non-red flag pathway.
  • Explicit LCD exclusions produced the strongest NOT_COVERED signals. Case 4 (DDD, surgery not under consideration) was denied on the explicit LCD exclusion at chars 4790–5060 — the model cited this verbatim and correctly identified it as the "single most decisive reason for denial." Case 10 (screening, asymptomatic) triggered five separate denial criteria, including the LCD's prohibition on retrospective validation by MRI abnormalities alone — a nuance that many human reviewers miss.
  • Clinical nuance surfaced without prompting. Case 1 proactively noted that contrast should be considered given possible metastatic disease — citing the specific LCD contrast indication at chars 5834–6737 without being asked. Case 9 correctly identified that "patient wants to see if anything has changed" constitutes patient preference, not clinical necessity, and cited the documentation requirement that the medical record must "support a contemplated diagnosis or treatment change derived from the MRI findings."
  • Citation character ranges are stable across cases. The same LCD passages were cited independently for analogous scenarios across multiple cases — for example, the red flag enumeration at chars 2927–3755 appeared in 7 of 10 cases. This suggests the Citations API produces reproducible attribution rather than stochastic passage selection, which matters for auditing consistency across large claim volumes.
Limitations

What this is not

Small evaluation set (10 cases)

Ten cases spans the major decision branches of L34220 but is insufficient to estimate real-world accuracy with confidence intervals. A production system requires hundreds of cases reviewed by independent clinicians.

Single procedure type

Lumbar MRI only. Prior auth automation at scale requires hundreds of procedure-specific LCDs and NCDs. Generalization to other procedures has not been tested.

Synthetic clinical notes

Cases were authored to clearly satisfy or fail LCD criteria. Real notes are messier — missing information, ambiguous timelines, inconsistent terminology. Performance on real-world notes may differ.

Researcher-labeled ground truth

Labels were assigned by the same researcher who designed the cases. Independent clinical review and inter-rater agreement (κ) computation are required before any production use claim.

Single jurisdiction

LCD L34220 applies to Noridian J-E and J-F (western US states). Coverage criteria vary by MAC jurisdiction. Determinations under other LCDs may differ for the same clinical scenario.

Not a clinical tool

This system has not been validated for clinical use, is not FDA-cleared, and must not be used to make or influence real prior authorization decisions. Research prototype on synthetic data only.
